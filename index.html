Problem statement 1 · AI surface defect detection

Find the defect while the coil can still be saved.

Surface defects start early — at the caster, the descaler, the reheat furnace — and a coil is graded only after every stage has been paid for. A strip running at 300 m/min cannot be inspected by eye.

This page is the detector, not a description of one. It names six surface defects in a photograph of steel, on your own machine. Nothing is uploaded.

Cause and recommended action appear here, one card per defect type, as soon as something is scored.

Why there are two confidence numbers A raw network score is not a probability. The one you read is corrected so that "0.40" means right about 40% of the time.

Probability is the calibrated number: an isotonic map fitted on the validation split turns the raw head score into something that means what it says. Raw score is what the network emitted. The table keeps them apart on purpose: .

Before calibration a box shown at "0.40" was right far less often than 40% of the time — expected calibration error 0.1418 on the held-out test split, against 0.0461 after. The map is monotone, so it cannot reorder detections: mAP50 is numerically unchanged at 0.707986 before and after. reports/calibration.json → metrics.ece, map50

The full calibration record — how the method was chosen out of fold, and what it does to each score.

How severity is decided, and where the advice comes from Severity scales the class's base tier by confidence and by how much of the frame the box covers. The advice is the mill knowledge base, unedited.

Severity is score_detection from src/inference.py — the same rule the Python console and the coil report use, so a band here means what it means there. Cause and recommended action are copied verbatim from that knowledge base; not one word of the metallurgy was written for this web page.

The confidence slider re-runs the decode and the non-maximum suppression on the cached output tensor rather than filtering the old list, because the threshold decides which candidates enter suppression in the first place. It does not re-run the model.

What the timing panel is, and is not One frame, one CPU thread, in a browser tab. It is a lower bound on the hardware, not a deployment frame rate.

The model figure is the forward pass alone, timed with performance.now(), excluding the warm-up call — that first run pays for code generation and memory arena allocation and is reported separately rather than folded in. The recorded headless run of this page measured —. A second recorded run over the same twelve frames measured a median of 49.1 ms, so treat it as a band rather than a figure — and the panel above measures it on your machine instead of repeating either. site/verify/parity_browser.json → summary.session_run_ms; site/verify/live_demo_check.json → browser_infer_ms

A wide strip costs several times a small frame because it is cut into three windows and the model runs three times. The line-speed arithmetic uses measured GPU throughput, not this.

03

Then we showed it clean steel, and it cried wolf

Every number above is on the kind of steel the model was trained on. A mill cares more about a different line, a different camera, and steel with nothing wrong with it. That was measured on 505 clean frames from a real production dataset. The result was bad.

Defect-free steel wrongly flagged as defective, on 505 clean frames:

Afterjoint fine-tune, same frames
32.5%

An inspector who is wrong nine times out of ten stops being read. Retraining on 1,190 certified-clean crops cut it to one in three — and every other measure improved with it.

Why this page still runs the worse model, on purpose The fixed model borrows four defect classes whose metallurgy is not published, so their severity tier cannot be set without inventing it.

The joint checkpoint scores better on both axes and it is still not shipped, deliberately. The Severstal release publishes a numeric mask value 1–4 and nothing else: no defect name, no metallurgical description, no grading rule. The severity tier that drives a coil's release decision cannot be set for those four classes without inventing metallurgy, and inventing it is the one thing this project will not do. They sit at the neutral middle tier with an explicit admission in place of a root cause, and the quality department owns the number. reports/severstal_dataset.md; src/inference.py SEVERSTAL_TIER

What actually fixed it — and the lesson to carry into a deployment Not a threshold and not a filter: the backbone had to be taught what clean steel looks like. Budget that for every new grade and every new camera.

1,190 certified-clean training crops, joint training on real target-domain positives and negatives, with the original class indices left untouched at 0–5 and four new classes appended at 6–9. Not a threshold change, not a post-hoc filter: the backbone learns that clean steel is clean. The features do not transfer on their own, so a per-line fine-tune with real negatives belongs in the plan and in the budget.

A separate out-of-scope gate also exists in the Python console: zero false rejections across 3,200 genuine steel frames, and 13 of 15 correct on a held-out suite of synthetic negatives. It is not wired into this page — which will happily score a photograph of a cat and tell you it found crazing. reports/ood_guard.json; reports/gap1_cross_domain_fix.md section 7

04

A real strip frame is 2048 × 1000. Squash it and the defect disappears.

The model reads a 256 × 256 square. Shrinking a whole line-scan frame into it squeezes the picture eight times across against four times down — a distortion the model never saw in training. So wide frames are cut into overlapping square windows instead. What that is worth, on two real frames:

Squashed into the square

0 defects

One pass over the whole frame, at the wrong aspect ratio. It finds nothing at all on the weld-line frame — and on the roll-mark frame it produces six confident boxes, every one of them labelled with the wrong defect.

Cut into three square windows

3 defects

Three passes at an even scale, merged once. Same frame, same model, same threshold. The cost is two extra forward passes, about 27 ms in total instead of 9 ms.

site/verify/strip_check.json → strips[strip_weldline].squash.n vs .tiled.n, and strips[strip_rollmark].squash.detections

Try it — the two dashed samples in the detector are those frames, unmodified.

How the tiled path was checked

Those two strip frames are also the honest failure case Their real defects are a roll mark and a weld line. This six-class model has neither name, so it answers with the nearest name it owns instead of refusing.

Across three tiled passes the roll-mark frame returns rolled-in_scale at raw 0.355 and inclusion at 0.270. The weld-line frame returns patches at 0.526 and 0.308 and scratches at 0.369. Every one of those five calls is confidently the wrong name, because the right name is not in this model's vocabulary — and none of them is a refusal. That is the 93.7% number in miniature, on a frame you can look at. site/verify/parity_browser.json → out_of_domain

05

What it does not do

Written to be believed rather than to be reassuring. Open any of them for the full measurement.

This is carbon steel, not stainless Every number on this page is measured on public benchmarks of hot-rolled carbon steel. Nothing has been measured on Jindal stainless strip.

NEU-DET is 1,800 grayscale 200×200 images of hot-rolled carbon steel, and the metallurgy of the surface is not the same as stainless. Everything here is a capability demonstration on a public benchmark, not a prediction of performance on your line.

One lighting rig, one camera The whole dataset was shot under a single imaging setup. The cross-domain section above is what happens when you ignore that.

Nothing has been measured on a different camera, a different illumination geometry or a different upstream mill — and the vision vendors themselves say the same defect class looks different from different mills. On steel from another line this model scored ROC AUC 0.608, which is chance. The per-line calibration protocol, not the architecture, is what decides whether this works.

Roll marks are measured weak, and edge cracks are only a proxy Roll marks score mean AP50 0.203 on 24 test instances. Edge cracks have no public dataset at all, so a geometric stand-in is used and is not called a crack detector.

On GC10-DET the roll-mark family (rolled_pit, crease) scores mean AP50 0.203 on 24 test instances, and four of eleven live rolled_pit images produce zero detections at conf 0.15. With 24 instances one flip moves recall 8–9 points, so that number is soft in both directions. Overall GC10 held-out test is mAP50 0.5821 — materially below the NEU-DET figure, on 333 images and 530 boxes.

No public dataset has an edge-crack class. The edge family here is crescent gap and waist fold — AP50 0.880 on 61 test instances, which is a strong result for edge geometry and not evidence about cracks. Calling it edge-crack detection would be a lie, so it is not called that. reports/gc10_coverage.json → brief_relevant_families; reports/gc10_dataset.md

Crazing recall is 0.224 If crazing is the defect that costs you money, this model is not yet the answer for it.

The weakest class on the weakest axis. 53 of 68 crazing misses are blind misses, not mislocalised ones. The per-class panel above explains why rather than averaging it away.

There are three different false-alarm numbers, not one A clean-crop proxy reads 23.7%, a legacy definition reads 59.4%, and the real clean-steel measurement reads 93.7%. The worst one is the one to trust.

The NEU-DET clean-crop proxy reads ≤23.7% (95% CI [17.7%, 30.1%]) but its effective sample size is 179 of 771 crops, and it ranges 5.8%–48.8% by source class. A legacy definition of the same thing reads 59.4%. The real clean-steel measurement — 93.7% before the fix, 32.5% after — is the one to trust, and it is much worse than the proxy suggested. reports/false_alarm.json; reports/gap1_cross_domain.json

The cost model is an assumption, not a measurement 5% defect prevalence and a 12:1 miss-to-false-alarm price are assumed. Every rupee figure is arithmetic on that assumption, printed with its inputs.

Only the product of those two assumptions is identifiable. The operating point conf 0.15 is set by a 90% detection floor, not by the economics. Read every money figure as a shape, not a forecast.

What this page in particular does not do It runs the six-class model, not the better ten-class one, and it has no out-of-scope gate — it will confidently score a photograph of anything.

The cross-domain false alarms above are live here. The out-of-scope gate is vendor/ood_guard.py in the Python console and has not been ported. The tiled path over a non-square frame has no Python reference, so the parity check covers the single-pass path only and the strip results are a browser-against-Node comparison. And it runs single-threaded on your CPU, so its timing is not the deployment timing. site/verify/strip_check.json; site/verify/parity_browser.json

Not measured, and therefore not claimed Seed variance, transfer across magnification, the anomaly channel and the closed loop back to the mill are proposed, not built.

Three seeds per architecture is about 6.5 h on this machine and would upgrade the nano-versus-small finding from "these weights" to "this architecture". The accuracy curve was measured on 200×200 crops, and a 320 px tile at 0.2 mm/px covers 64 mm of strip. The full limitations section carries all of this at length.
